The last release added agents and app-to-app actions. This one adds almost no
features at all, on purpose.

We captured all twenty apps plus the shell and Settings, in light and dark, and
then did the boring part: read the images one at a time and wrote down every
defect. That produced a hundred and nine findings. The useful discovery was not
any single one of them — it was that most were instances of a much smaller number
of causes. So the fixes went to the causes.

## What you will actually notice

**Nothing overlaps any more.** Dashboard widgets now sit on a bounded stage that
refuses to place a widget it cannot place legally, and hands you a tray listing
what it refused. Before, a widget could land on top of your app icons, or below
the fold where it was both invisible and impossible to remove.

**Menus and popovers are opaque.** You could read your dashboard through an open
menu. Both shared overlay surfaces had been repaired once already on this same
complaint and were still translucent enough to read a tile icon through.

**Menus open where you clicked.** A menu opened on an object's title used to open
inside the 44-pixel header band and slice the title it was opened on.

**Labels you can read.** A text label painted on the accent fill measured under the
contrast a label needs — on every theme, in some cases as low as 1.3 to 1 for a
disabled control that faded its fill and its glyph together. There is now one
shared accent face, and a check that fails the build if anything paints a label on
the decorative accent again.

**Settings is navigable.** Twenty-two items in one flat list became six labelled
groups.

## Your second device keeps up

Pairing used to be a handshake that did not move much. Now the objects that
existed before the two devices met are backfilled onto the newly paired device,
and anything you create afterwards fans out to every paired device on its own.

Joining is also safe in a way it was not. A device joining a vault adopts that
vault's identity, and refuses to re-point a vault that is already in use — the
previous behaviour could leave the joining device unable to open its own vault.

## Lock an object and mean it

Read-only was painted on the chrome. The editor refused to write and several other
paths wrote anyway: a locked contact could be merged out of existence, a locked
book was written on open, and body editors ignored the lock entirely.

The lock is now enforced at the write, everywhere, and Lock and Unlock live in the
object `⋯` menu that every app shares. Files, Books, Contacts and Database can set
it, not merely respect it. Every guard has a test that fails when the guard is
deleted, which is how we found that four of the previous release's lock tests
passed with the guard removed.

## Smaller things

A collapsed sidebar now leaves the tab order instead of trapping focus in a panel
you cannot see. Graph node labels get a chip so they read against the tangle they
float over. Files list columns line up on one shared icon track. Solheim joins the
inference providers you can bring your own key to. And if a document lost content,
the app tells you rather than showing you a blank page that looks fine.

macOS builds are signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so they open
without a Gatekeeper warning. Windows is currently unsigned, so you may see a
SmartScreen "unknown publisher" prompt, where the option you want is **More info**
then **Run anyway**. Existing installs update in-app from **Settings → Updates**.

It is still a beta, so keep backups of anything important. Your vault is a plain
folder you control completely.
